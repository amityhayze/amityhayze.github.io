Listening report // SR–001

Steve O Creep — Love and Death

Artist: Steve O Creep 22 tracks Album review Full Transmission

For this edition of Full Transmission, presented by Send the Track, I'm taking a look at Love and Death, a 22-track project by Steve O Creep that blends underground rap with alternative influences.

Rather than focusing on individual tracks alone, I wanted to take a closer look at the project as a whole, including the production, vocal performances, mixing, mastering, and the creative decisions behind the music. Sometimes the most interesting parts of an album are the things you notice after spending time with it, especially when you start thinking about how all the pieces fit together.

Steve handles the production himself, along with the mixing and mastering, and the project features contributions from artists including 85 Stew, Doc Onesome, Oddwhut, DJ Dirty 2, and Bca Creep.

01 / Production and Pacing

One of the first things that stood out to me was the pacing. With 22 tracks, there's a lot of room for a project to lose momentum, but I really enjoy when an album keeps me engaged by giving me something different to focus on.

Love and Death has a lot of variety in its production, moving between melodic singing, rapping, and more aggressive vocal performances. That variety helps the project feel like more than a collection of songs using the same formula.

The changes in delivery, production choices, and vocal effects give individual tracks their own character. The layering and effects are particularly interesting because they help distinguish the different approaches throughout the album.

As someone who produces, records, mixes, and masters my own music, I always find it interesting to hear how another artist approaches the entire process themselves. When one person is responsible for so many parts of a project, those decisions can become closely connected. Production choices affect how a vocal sits in the mix, and the way a vocal is performed can influence how the instrumental needs to support it.

Question for Steve Steve, how does being responsible for the entire process affect your sound? Do you think you approach mixing differently when you're also the person creating the instrumental?

02 / The Contrast Between Sound and Subject Matter

Something else that stood out to me was the contrast between the energy of the music and the subject matter.

Love and Death touches on heartbreak, regret, religion, death, wanting help, and forgiveness. Tracks like "Dear God," "Regret," and "Please Forgive Me Clark" bring those heavier themes to the forefront.

What I find interesting is the tension between the production, which is constantly moving and evolving, and the subject matter, which is often much darker and more vulnerable. The music doesn't always have to sound as heavy as the emotions being expressed. Sometimes that contrast makes the subject matter more interesting because the listener has to reconcile what they're hearing sonically with what the artist is communicating.

I also think this contrast gives the album room to move between different emotional states without relying on one sound to communicate everything. The variety in vocal delivery and production helps each track approach its subject matter differently.

Question for Steve Steve, how do those themes connect to the title Love and Death? Did you have that central idea in mind from the beginning, or did the meaning develop as you made the album?

03 / Favorite Tracks and Standout Moments

With 22 tracks, choosing favorites wasn't easy, but several songs stood out to me: "What If It's Me," "Pills on Her Tongue," "Crossing Over," "Dear God," "Regret," "Death Pt. 1," and "Death Pt. 2."

I also really enjoyed the final stretch between "Death Pt. 1" and "Death Pt. 2." The way those tracks sit alongside one another made me curious about whether they were intended to function as a connected ending rather than simply two separate songs.

I think album sequencing can be an underrated part of making a project. Individual songs matter, obviously, but the order in which they appear can change how a listener interprets them. When tracks share a title, theme, or musical idea, their placement can create a relationship that extends beyond either song on its own.

That final stretch made me wonder whether the two tracks were conceived together or whether their connection became clearer during the process of making the album.

Question for Steve Steve, did you envision "Death Pt. 1" and "Death Pt. 2" as a connected ending? Is there a particular relationship between those tracks that listeners should pick up on?

04 / Final Transmission

One of the things I appreciate about Love and Death is the range of approaches across the project. The production, vocal delivery, and subject matter give the album several different points of entry, while the recurring themes of heartbreak, regret, faith, and forgiveness give listeners something to follow across the 22 tracks.

For me, the most interesting part of listening was looking beyond the songs individually and considering the decisions connecting them: how the production supports different vocal performances, how the emotional themes interact with the sound, and how the sequencing shapes the experience of listening to the project as a whole.

I'd like to thank Steve for sharing Love and Death with me and giving me a closer look at the creative process behind it. Getting to hear not just the finished music but also the thinking behind it is one of the things that makes these conversations worthwhile.

I'm Amity Hayze, and this has been Full Transmission, presented by Send the Track.
